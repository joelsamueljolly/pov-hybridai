Rent the models. Own the meaning.

Why energy utilities need a hybrid AI strategy, not an LLM strategy, for their customer core processes. Billing audit and exception management is the use case we worked through in detail.

LLMs love to yap. A customer process doesn't need a confident essay. It needs a decision.

Take a billing exception. Is this bill at risk? What kind of exception is it? Who owns it? What evidence supports the adjustment? The same questions come up in onboarding, payments, collections and disputes. “Not everything needs an LLM” is only half the insight. The other half is that not everything should be owned by the model vendor either.

Rent
General-purpose intelligence

Foundation models, specialised anomaly and forecasting models, decision-model services, document intelligence, vector search.

Own
Domain meaning

Tariff logic, contract terms, meter-data semantics, case taxonomy, thresholds, audit trail and approval rights.

Selective sovereignty

Change vendors without changing what “valid bill”, “high-risk case” or “approved adjustment” means.

Customer processes are not a chat problem

High volume, high consequence and regulated. They need typed, auditable decisions, not prose.

LLMs are collaborators, not sovereigns

They draft, summarise, retrieve and propose. Owned rules and accountable people decide.

The hybrid stack

Five layers, one division of labour, and a person who is accountable

Pick a layer. Order matters: rules and physics set the non-negotiables, and the LLM sits late as an interface to language and ambiguity.

Physics-informed approaches combine data with first principles, which embeds physical constraints and improves interpretability. Hybrid energy-system research also treats the LLM as an interface, not the final decision-maker.

Customer core processes

The same pattern runs across the customer core

Every core process mixes exact rules, pattern detection, narrow judgments, language work and human accountability. Pick a process to see who does what.

Worked use case · Billing audit and exceptions

Billing exceptions: the experiment we detailed

Billing is where volume, regulation and customer trust meet, so it makes a good test. LLMs should not own the exception lifecycle. They improve its language, context and reasoning interfaces.

Thresholds are policy, not configuration

A probability is not a decision. It is an input to a decision policy. Pick an exception class and move the threshold. The cheapest setting differs by class, so one global “0.8” is wrong for most of them.

0.50
Cases scoring at or above the threshold go to review. Simulated data for illustration, not a benchmark.

Where narrow decision models fit

Use one when the question is narrow, the input can be flattened and the cost of a wrong answer is understood: an exception gate, a duplicate-invoice check, a zero-read data-quality check, queue triage, an evidence check, a model router, and a judge of LLM output (“does this customer explanation contain an unsupported billing claim?”). As I argued in my earlier articles and posts on decision models as judges, break the job into plain yes-or-no checks. A vague “review this response” or a complex ranking task performs close to random. Recalibrate thresholds per task and per model version, and run in shadow mode while you do.
